# Hidden on Earth — Art credits & image sources

Attribution and image provenance for **Hidden on Earth — Seek & Find**, a hidden-object game for kids
played on famous paintings.

Every classic painting in the game is a **public-domain artwork**, taken either from a museum's
**CC0 / open-access** programme or from **Wikimedia Commons**. The three pictures in *Your collection*
are **original works by Dünya Üçer**, used with the family's permission.

## Why these are free to use

1. **The artworks are in the public domain.** Copyright in a painting lasts, at most, for the
   artist's life plus 70 years. The most recent artists here — Rousseau (d. 1910) and Cézanne
   (d. 1906) — passed that threshold decades ago, so every painting is in the public domain
   worldwide.
2. **Faithful photographs of flat artworks add no new copyright.** A straight, faithful photographic
   reproduction of a 2-D public-domain work does not create a fresh copyright in the United States
   (*Bridgeman Art Library v. Corel*, 1999) or in the EU (Directive 2019/790, Article 14). So the
   high-resolution scans are free to use as well.
3. **Sources are chosen to avoid even contractual friction.** Where a museum runs a formal
   open-access programme the image is taken from there and is dedicated to the public domain under
   **CC0** (Art Institute of Chicago, The Met) or released as public-domain open data (National
   Gallery of Art, Rijksmuseum). The remaining works are taken from **Wikimedia Commons**, where the
   public-domain status is documented on each file page. This sidesteps the "terms of use" fine print
   that some museum websites attach to their own downloads (a contractual matter, separate from
   copyright).

### Caveats

- This is general information, **not legal advice**. For commercial App Store distribution, a lawyer's
  sign-off is the only way to be 100% certain.
- A museum's *website terms of use* can restrict downloads from that site even when the underlying
  artwork is public domain. Using the CC0 / open-access sources or Wikimedia Commons above keeps us
  clear of that.
- Dünya Üçer's drawings are **not** public domain — they are the family's, used by permission.
