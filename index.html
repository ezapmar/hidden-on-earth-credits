Three steps, and none of them needs reading.

Pick a painting

Rows by theme: animals, winter, night, the sea. Each card says how many things are hiding in it.

Look and tap

The painting fills the screen and pinches to four times its size. The cards below show what to look for, and tapping one says its name aloud.

Keep the sticker

Finishing a painting earns a detail cut from it. The sticker book has one slot for each painting.

Three rules the game is built on.

Nothing is drawn on top

Every thing to find is a real detail of the artwork. The monkey on Seurat's riverbank has been there since 1884.

Every count is exact

If the game says three dogs, the painting has three dogs. A thing we cannot count for certain is left out.

No clock, nothing to lose

No timer, no score, no lives. A wrong tap makes a soft ripple. The hint pulses on one hiding place; the child still has to see it.

It was made for one child.

He is six, he likes looking for things, and he is learning English. That is why there is no clock, why the taps are forgiving and why every word is said aloud. Four of his own drawings are in the game beside Van Gogh and Hokusai, credited to Dünya Üçer, 2026.

The icon is an eye inside a magnifying glass, and the eye is the Anatolian kilim motif that looks back. The game asks for the same thing: look at one painting for longer than you meant to.
